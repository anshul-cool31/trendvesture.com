Minimalism in fashion is frequently misunderstood as a restrictive mandate to own only fifteen beige garments. In reality, sartorial minimalism is about freedom: liberating yourself from the cognitive fatigue of decision paralysis, clutter, and unfulfilling impulse shopping.

When your wardrobe contains only pieces you genuinely love, that fit your current body, and that harmonize effortlessly, getting dressed becomes a joyful daily ritual.

---

## 1. The Great Wardrobe Audit

Begin by emptying your entire closet onto your bed. Categorize every piece into four clear piles:
- **Keep:** Pieces you have worn and loved in the past ninety days that fit comfortably right now.
- **Tailor / Repair:** High-quality garments that require minor hem alterations or button replacements to be wearable.
- **Donate / Resell:** Pieces in good condition that no longer align with your aesthetic or lifestyle.
- **Recycle:** Worn-out textiles past their usable life.

---

## 2. Defining Your Core Neutral Palette

Select two anchor neutrals (e.g., black and charcoal, or navy and camel) and one secondary light neutral (e.g., crisp white, ivory, or oatmeal). Ensuring that 80% of your wardrobe foundations conform to this cohesive palette guarantees that almost any top and bottom combination will match seamlessly.
